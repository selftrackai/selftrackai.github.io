When School Is Out of Reach

Saheb wants to learn, and there is no school for him to walk into. This page looks at why that happens, and what the data says.

Six barriers

Usually it is not one thing that keeps a child out of school. It is several small things together.

Distance

If the nearest school is far, or there is none, a child has no way to start. In the chapter Saheb says there is no school in his area.

Financial pressure

Uniforms, books and transport add up, and so does the earning a child gives up by studying.

The need to work

When a household lives day to day, a child’s earnings can decide whether the family eats.

Little awareness or support

If nobody around has been to school, it is hard to picture what it can lead to, or how to enrol.

Infrastructure

Schools need teachers, buildings and safe ways to reach them. Where those are missing, families give up.

Hard to continue

Even after starting, a child who falls behind or has to earn may drop out.

Back to Saheb

Saheb’s story shows every barrier in one place. There is no school nearby. His family lives from what he and others find in the garbage. And when someone offers to start a school, it is a promise that never becomes real.

What makes it sad is that he clearly wants to learn. The chapter shows a boy who is ready, and a system that is not.

The law. The Constitution of India includes Article 21A, which gives children aged 6 to 14 the right to free and compulsory education. It is put into practice through the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009. The gap between the promise and what Saheb experiences is one of the questions this project asks. (Check the exact wording on the Government of India’s legislative database before quoting.)
