Mukesh

“I Want to Drive a Car”

AuthorAnees JungSettingFirozabad, Uttar PradeshCharacterMukesh, a bangle-maker’s son

A town that makes bangles

Firozabad is known for its glass bangles. In the chapter it is also a town where a whole way of life is passed from parents to children.

Mukesh’s family, like many others in Firozabad, has been in the bangle business for generations. Children grow up watching their parents join and colour the glass rings, and it feels natural for them to join in.

The author visits the homes and workshops. She describes rooms without much light or air, and furnaces burning nearby. She says the work can harm the eyes, and that many young workers lose some of their sight before they grow up.

Yet the chapter does not blame the families. It points to a chain of people who gain from the trade, such as moneylenders, middlemen and officials, and to a feeling among the workers that this is simply their fate.

Then there is Mukesh, who says he wants to be a motor mechanic. He talks about the garage as if it were something he could walk to. It is a small dream, and the chapter treats it with a lot of respect.

What shapes Mukesh’s days

Place

Firozabad

A glass-making town where bangles are the main trade and many homes are also workshops.

Family

Bangle-making families

Skills and loans both pass down. Children learn the craft at home.

Work

A traditional occupation

It is a proud craft, but the chapter shows how little of the profit reaches the makers.

Childhood

Child labour

Children help with the work because the household needs every pair of hands.

Money

Poverty

Debt and low pay keep families in the same place, generation after generation.

Safety

Unsafe conditions

Heat, dust and poor light are part of the workday, as the author describes it.

For a health or safety claim beyond what the chapter says, see the sources on the research page. I have not added numbers about Firozabad here because I could not verify them yet.

The furnace and the bangles

This is the world Mukesh was born into. The glow is warm, but the work is hard.

Tools he wants to learn

A mechanic’s wrench, a hammer and a gear. His idea is a different kind of skill.

A car

For him, a car is a symbol of ambition. It stands for going somewhere.

The road ahead

A dream is only the start. It needs training, safety and a fair chance to become real.

Why the car matters

In the story Mukesh does not ask for a rich life. He asks to learn a skill and to work in a garage. The car is important because it represents movement, and everything around him tells him to stay in place.

I think that is the sharpest contrast in the chapter. Saheb’s dream is school. Mukesh’s dream is a trade. Both dreams are modest, and both are at risk.

Source for all points on this page: Anees Jung, “Lost Spring”, in Flamingo, Class XII English (NCERT), paraphrased.
