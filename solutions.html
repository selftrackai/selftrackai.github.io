From Awareness to Action

Anees Jung asks questions. This page is where I try to answer some of them, as a student and not as an expert.

If I could change one thing…

These are my own reflections. The author does not make these suggestions in the chapter.

Saheb’s biggest problem is that there is no school near him. Building schools is not enough. They also need teachers who turn up, safe routes and lessons that make sense to children who work.

If a child’s earnings pay for dinner, telling the family to stop will not work by itself. Help with food, loans and steady adult jobs makes it possible for a child to stay in class.

India already has laws against employing young children. What Mukesh’s town shows is that a law on paper needs inspections, support for families and action against those who profit.

Mukesh does not want a fortune. He wants to train as a mechanic. Apprenticeships and skill programmes for older children, with safe conditions, can turn a small dream into a career.

Clean water, drainage and secure homes make daily life easier. They also give families proof of belonging, which makes it simpler to enrol children in school.

These are student reflections, not research findings. For evidence, use the sources on the research page.

Who can do what

Change needs more than one kind of effort. Tap each group.

  • Education access: schools within reach, and support to stay enrolled
  • Infrastructure: water, sanitation and safe housing
  • Family support: income and food support so children do not have to earn
  • Child-labour protection: inspections, rescue and rehabilitation
  • Scholarships and fee support
  • Remedial classes for children who have missed school
  • Counselling and follow-up when a child stops coming
  • Education programmes such as evening classes and libraries
  • Skill development for teenagers and parents
  • Community initiatives that welcome children into school
  • Do not employ children, even for small jobs
  • Support legitimate educational initiatives
  • Treat workers with dignity
  • Spread awareness, starting with the people around you
