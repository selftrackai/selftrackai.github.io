Two boys, two towns

Saheb in Seemapuri. Mukesh in Firozabad.

One thing in common

Poverty, work, and a wish for something else.

Morning

Dreams, education and hope, still growing.

The two stories are not only about poverty; they are about children whose choices, education and dreams are shaped by circumstances beyond their control.

What I take from the chapter

Saheb and Seemapuri

A boy whose sack was his own, until a wage made it someone else’s. His wish for school shows what is missing.

Mukesh and Firozabad

A boy in a family trade who dares to want a different one. His car stands for movement.

Education and child labour

School is the way out, but only if it is reachable. Child labour is a symptom of things that need fixing at the root.

Poverty

It decides much of what a child can do. It is a condition, not a fault.

Dreams

The most striking thing about both boys is that they still have one.

Hope

Small and practical: a school nearby, a skill to learn, a fair chance.

Understanding is the first step. Change begins when opportunity becomes accessible.
