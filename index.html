What is Lost Spring?

A chapter in two halves, about two boys who should be at school and are not.

Two boys, two towns, and one question: who decides what a child gets to become?

“Lost Spring” is a piece of reportage by Anees Jung, and it is in our Class XII English book, Flamingo. It comes from her larger book, Lost Spring: Stories of Stolen Childhood, where she writes about children she met in different parts of India who were working when they should have been learning and playing.

In this chapter she follows two of them. The first is Saheb, who picks rags in Seemapuri on the edge of Delhi. The second is Mukesh, who is born into a family of bangle-makers in Firozabad.

What makes the chapter stay with you is that neither boy is a villain or a hero. They are ordinary children, and their days are decided by poverty, family, and the places they were born into.

The title works as an image. Spring is the season when things begin to grow. When childhood is “lost”, that season is taken away before it starts. This site explores that idea through the stories, the facts around them, and a few thoughts of my own.
